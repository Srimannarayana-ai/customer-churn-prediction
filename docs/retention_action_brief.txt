Retention action brief
Customer Churn Retention Scorecard
=================================

If I were supporting a retention / CRM team with this scorecard, I would not
treat Churn_Prob as a final "fire this customer" score. I would use it to
decide who to contact first and which offer levers to test. Based on the
current build, here are five concrete next steps.

1) Start with the 1,267 "high risk / high value" customers
   These have predicted churn probability at or above 50% and monthly charges
   at or above the median (~$70). About two-thirds in this bucket have already
   churned in the historical label — the rest are the live save opportunities.
   Estimated annual revenue represented on the full focus list is about $1.49M
   (MonthlyCharges * 12 summed). I would export this list to the outbound
   team with Contract, TechSupport, and InternetService attached.

2) Lead with Month-to-month + no Tech Support messaging
   Month-to-month contracts churn at about 42.7% vs ~11% (one year) and ~3%
   (two year). Customers without Tech Support churn at about 41.6% vs ~15%
   with support. That is the clearest product story in the data: convert
   flexible contracts where possible, and bundle support for fiber / heavy
   streaming accounts on the focus list.

3) Treat "elevated risk (top 20%)" as a nurture watch list
   248 customers sit in the top risk quintile without clearing the high-value
   bar. Average monthly spend is lower (~$54). Use cheaper digital nudges
   (email / in-app) before expensive agent time.

4) Prioritize early-tenure saves
   Tenure 0-12 months shows ~47% churn vs ~9.5% in the 49-72 band. A 30-day
   onboarding checklist for new month-to-month fiber customers will usually
   beat a late-stage win-back campaign.

5) Ask for one operational owner per action type
   - CRM / outbound owns the high risk / high value call list
   - Product owns Tech Support attach and contract conversion experiments
   - Finance / analytics owns weekly refresh of Churn_Prob and focus flags
   The scorecard's job is prioritization. The action plan needs named owners
   and a 30/60/90-day check on the same customerIDs.

What I would not do
- Blast every predicted churner with the same discount (destroys margin)
- Report accuracy alone in a stakeholder deck (show precision / recall / AUC)
- Pretend coefficients are causal without an experiment

Numbers above come from the cleaned customer table and model outputs in this
project (focus list = 1,515 customers: 1,267 high risk / high value,
248 elevated risk).
